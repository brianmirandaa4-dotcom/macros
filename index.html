            The method

            Why your macros change during a cut

            1. Phase 1 · Trim fat, hold carbs. Early on the deficit comes mostly out of fat while carbs stay put to fuel training. Fat starts near 30% of calories and comes down to a floor of 20% (never under 0.25 g per lb). The ACSM sports nutrition position statement advises athletes against chronic intakes below 20%.
            2. Protein rises the whole time. The leaner you get and the bigger the deficit, the more protein protects muscle. With body fat % entered, protein goes from 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of lean mass (Helms, Aragon & Fitschen, JISSN 2014). Without it, 1.8 to 2.4 g per kg of body weight (Roberts et al., 2020). Carbs are held steady while protein climbs.
            3. Phase 2 · Lower carbs. Once fat hits its floor, every further cut comes from carbs. Syncope warns if carbs fall under 2 g per kg, the low end for lifters (Roberts et al.). Lean or past week 8, a weekly refeed day is suggested: one training day with extra carbs, bringing that day to about maintenance.
            4. Diet breaks and recovery. An optional week at maintenance every 8 weeks gives you a mental and hormonal reset; research on structured breaks (e.g. the MATADOR trial) found they don't hurt fat loss. After the cut, 4 recovery weeks step calories back up. Fat climbs back toward 28%, carbs rise, and protein eases to 2.2 g per kg of lean mass.
            5. Pace. Aim for 0.5–1% of body weight lost per week to keep muscle (Helms 2014). Slower, around 0.5%, is better the leaner you are. Bulks aim for 0.25–0.5% gained per week with protein around 1.8 g per kg (Iraki et al., 2019).

            This applies published guidelines to your numbers. It's not individual medical advice, so adjust based on your weigh-ins, energy and training.
